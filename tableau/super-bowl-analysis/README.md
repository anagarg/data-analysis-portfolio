# Super Bowl Analysis

## Overview
This project analyzes Super Bowl data to explore trends in television viewership, advertising costs, game competitiveness, and halftime performances. The analysis combines multiple datasets in Tableau to examine how characteristics of the Super Bowl relate to audience engagement and advertising value.

The project was completed in Tableau as part of the Global Career Accelerator Data Analytics program.

## Business Question
How have Super Bowl television ratings and advertising costs changed over time, and what factors may influence the value and audience reach of Super Bowl advertising?

## Data
The analysis combines three datasets:
- Super Bowl Scores: Game number, winning and losing teams, final score, venue, city, attendance, and season information.
- TV Ratings: Average U.S. viewers, household ratings and shares, demographic ratings, and advertising costs.
- Halftime Shows: Super Bowl number, performing musicians, headliner status, and number of songs performed.

Combining these datasets allowed the analysis to examine relationships between game outcomes, television audiences, advertising costs, and halftime entertainment.

## Analysis
I created Tableau visualizations to analyze Super Bowl trends from multiple perspectives.

Key analysis included:
- Tracking average household television ratings by year
- Examining changes in Super Bowl advertising costs over time
- Comparing TV ratings and advertising costs
- Analyzing average U.S. viewership by halftime show headliner
- Examining average viewership based on game competitiveness
- Splitting game scores into winning and losing scores using Tableau calculated fields
- Calculating the score difference between teams
- Classifying games into four result categories:
  - Nail-Biter
  - Close Game
  - Easy Win
  - Blowout
- Combining information from multiple datasets to create a broader view of Super Bowl audience and advertising trends

## Key Findings
### Television Ratings
Average household ratings varied considerably across the history of the Super Bowl. The dataset shows household ratings ranging from approximately 18.5 to 49.1, demonstrating substantial variation in the size of the television audience over time.

The analysis also shows that ratings generally reached much higher levels during several periods in the earlier history of the Super Bowl, while more recent games have generally had lower household ratings.

### Advertising Costs
The cost of a Super Bowl advertisement increased dramatically over time. The dataset ranges from approximately $37,500 for advertising in the earliest Super Bowls to $7 million for Super Bowl LVII.

This substantial increase highlights the growing commercial value of Super Bowl advertising despite changes in television ratings over time.

### Advertising Cost vs. TV Ratings
The comparison between household ratings and advertising costs does not show a strong direct relationship in the dataset. While advertising costs increased substantially over time, household ratings did not increase at the same rate.

This suggests that the value of a Super Bowl advertising placement is influenced by more than simply the household rating of the game.

### Game Competitiveness
Games were classified according to the difference between the winning and losing team's scores:
- Nail-Biter: 7 points or fewer
- Close Game: 8–14 points
- Easy Win: 15–21 points
- Blowout: More than 21 points

The analysis can then be used to compare average U.S. viewership across these different types of games, providing insight into whether more competitive games tend to attract larger audiences.
