# Winter Sports Interest Analysis

## Overview

This project analyzes relative Google search interest for nine winter sports in the United States from 2004 through early 2022.

The analysis examines seasonal patterns, recurring Olympic-year spikes, differences in search interest between sports, and the overall popularity of individual sports. Excel charts, formulas, and comparative analysis were used to identify patterns in search behavior.

The project was completed as part of the Global Career Accelerator Data Analytics program.

## Business Questions
This analysis explores several questions:
- How does search interest in winter sports change over time?
- Do winter sports show recurring seasonal patterns?
- How does search interest change during Winter Olympic years?
- How do search trends differ between sports?
- Which winter sports have the highest overall search interest?
- What events or factors may help explain unusually large changes in search interest?

## Data
The dataset contains monthly relative Google search interest for nine winter sports in the United States.

The analysis covers **January 2004 through March 2022**.

The sports analyzed are:
- Curling
- Luge
- Speed Skating
- Nordic Combined
- Biathlon
- Alpine Skiing
- Bobsleigh
- Hockey
- Snowboarding

### Understanding Search Interest
The dataset uses a relative search-interest scale from **0 to 100**.

A value of 100 represents the month and search term with the highest proportional search volume in the dataset. Other values are scaled relative to that maximum.

For example, a search-interest value of 50 represents approximately half the proportional search volume of the highest month-and-sport combination.

Because these are relative search-interest values, they should not be interpreted as the actual number of Google searches.

## Analysis

### 1. Curling Search Interest Over Time
I created a line chart to examine the trend in search interest for curling from 2004 through 2022.

The analysis identified a strong cyclical pattern:
- Smaller increases in interest occur during the winter season.
- Much larger increases occur during Winter Olympic years.
- Major recurring peaks appear approximately every four years.

The largest curling peak in the dataset occurred in **February 2018**, when search interest reached **96**.

The pattern is consistent with curling receiving increased attention during the winter season and particularly during the Winter Olympics.

### 2. Using AI to Develop a Hypothesis
I used ChatGPT to predict when search interest in curling would likely be highest and compared that prediction with the trends observed in the visualization.

The prediction aligned with the observed pattern of increased interest during winter months and particularly during Winter Olympic years.

I then refined the question to obtain more specific information about individual years, months, and geographic differences.

This produced additional hypotheses about factors that could contribute to changes in search interest.

### 3. Limitations and Potential Biases
The analysis also evaluated limitations associated with using Google search data and AI-generated explanations.

The dataset measures **Google search interest**, rather than total public interest in a sport.

Potential limitations include:
- Google search behavior may not represent all audiences.
- Search behavior varies by country and region.
- People who primarily use other search engines may be underrepresented.
- Search terms and languages can affect the measured results.
- Explanations generated by AI may contain assumptions or biases that are not directly supported by the dataset.

These limitations were considered when interpreting the results.

### 4. Luge Search Interest
I created a second line chart to compare luge with curling.

Luge showed a similar four-year Olympic cycle, but its Olympic-year peaks were generally much smaller than curling's.

One major exception occurred in **February 2010**, when luge search interest reached **100**, the highest value in the entire dataset for luge.

The workbook's analysis connects this unusually large spike with a fatal training accident that occurred shortly before the 2010 Winter Olympics.

This demonstrates that search interest can be affected by major events beyond normal seasonal and Olympic patterns.

### 5. Hockey and Snowboarding
I created a combined line chart comparing hockey and snowboarding.

Both sports showed recurring seasonal patterns, with search interest generally increasing during winter months and decreasing during warmer months.

However, their longer-term patterns differed:
- **Hockey** showed a relatively consistent yearly cycle without major Olympic-year spikes.
- **Snowboarding** showed both a seasonal winter pattern and additional increases during Winter Olympic years.

This suggests that Olympic exposure has a stronger visible effect on search interest for some sports than others.

### 6. Comparing Overall Popularity
I calculated the average search interest for five sports that had not yet been compared:
- Speed Skating
- Nordic Combined
- Biathlon
- Alpine Skiing
- Bobsleigh

I then created a bar chart comparing their average interest levels.

Among these five sports, **Bobsleigh had the highest average search interest**.

The average search-interest values were:

| Sport | Average Search Interest |
|---|---:|
| Bobsleigh | 1.38 |
| Speed Skating | 1.13 |
| Alpine Skiing | 1.05 |
| Biathlon | 0.66 |
| Nordic Combined | 0.42 |

### 7. Olympic-Year Comparison
As a LevelUp analysis, I compared search interest for Speed Skating, Alpine Skiing, Nordic Combined, Biathlon, and Bobsleigh during February of Winter Olympic years.

The Olympic years included:
- 2006
- 2010
- 2014
- 2018
- 2022

A horizontal stacked bar chart was created to compare search-interest levels across the Olympic years.

This comparison provides a more focused view of how interest in these sports changes during the month when the Winter Olympics are taking place.

## Key Findings
- Winter sports generally show increased search interest during the winter season.
- Curling shows a particularly strong four-year cycle corresponding with Winter Olympic years.
- Curling reached a search-interest value of **96 in February 2018**.
- Luge reached **100 in February 2010**, its highest value in the dataset.
- Hockey shows a recurring seasonal pattern but comparatively less variation between Olympic and non-Olympic years.
- Snowboarding shows both recurring winter seasonality and additional Olympic-year increases.
- Among Speed Skating, Nordic Combined, Biathlon, Alpine Skiing, and Bobsleigh, **Bobsleigh had the highest average search interest at approximately 1.38**.
- Across the full set of sports analyzed, **Hockey had the highest average search interest at approximately 13.97**, followed by Snowboarding at approximately 9.17 and Curling at approximately 3.07.
- Search-interest spikes can be associated with both recurring events, such as the Winter Olympics, and unusual events that attract additional public attention.

## Insights for Content and Marketing Strategy
The observed search patterns could be useful for organizations promoting winter sports content.

### Seasonal Timing
Interest generally increases during winter months, suggesting that content and marketing campaigns can be aligned with periods of naturally higher search interest.

### Olympic-Year Opportunities
The recurring increases during Winter Olympic years indicate that Olympic coverage can create additional opportunities for audience engagement.

### Sport-Specific Strategies
Different sports show different levels of seasonality and Olympic sensitivity.

For example, hockey shows relatively consistent seasonal interest, while curling and snowboarding show more pronounced Olympic-year increases.

This suggests that promotional strategies could be tailored to the specific search behavior of each sport rather than applying the same seasonal strategy across all sports.
